# Device Arrival animation direction

The HUD uses one shared police-terminal shell and gives every hardware family its own physical entrance. The device is always readable before the supporting text appears.

## Saumya's AirPods Pro — CASE LAUNCH

1. A charging case rises from below the HUD and overshoots its resting position.
2. The lid hinges open and the two buds spring out on staggered arcs.
3. Cyan radio rings scan outward; a gold pixel lands on the active battery segment.
4. Departure reverses the choreography: rings collapse, buds corkscrew home, and the lid snaps shut.

## Saumya's XM4 — SIGNAL LOCK

1. The folded headphones rotate into frame with the ear cups tucked inward.
2. The band expands and the cups punch outward with a bass-like recoil.
3. Paired cyan and violet waves lock around the cups while the status text resolves.
4. Departure folds both cups inward, breaks the signal into pixels, and spins the silhouette away.

## ProtoArc K100-A — KEY WAVE

1. The keyboard drops in as a dark silhouette with a small landing squash.
2. Four rows of keys ignite left-to-right in a cyan, blue, violet, gold wave.
3. Three keystrokes pop upward as bright square particles and settle back into the board.
4. Departure extinguishes the keys right-to-left and lowers the keyboard below the terminal frame.

All motion is computed from a deterministic timeline so production rendering, preview snapshots, and tests use the same animation model.
